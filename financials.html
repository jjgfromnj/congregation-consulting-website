Reports the finance committee can act on.

Many congregations are running a financial framework designed two decades ago for software they no longer use. The numbers come out, but nobody quite gets them.

What I work on

Chart of accounts

Rebuilt so that funds, restricted gifts, dues categories, and program revenue land where they belong — and so the statement you hand the board answers the question they're actually asking.

Dues, pledges and billing

Consistent treatment of dues, fair-share arrangements, abatements, and pledges, so aging reports mean something and the annual renewal cycle doesn't generate a month of corrections.

QuickBooks cleanup

Many QuickBooks files carry years of legacy structure: accounts added one at a time, categories that overlap, an income statement nobody can read. I help clients:

  • Reconfigure the chart of accounts, including parent and sub-accounts, so reports answer the questions your board actually asks.
  • Streamline by retiring unused accounts, removing duplicates, and simplifying what staff have to choose from.
  • Consolidate accounts that drifted apart over the years into one consistent framework.
  • Update legacy financial information for modern synagogue needs: dues, pledges, restricted funds, and school and High Holiday income.

I also do this work for organizations outside the synagogue world.

QuickBooks and your platform

Extensive hands-on experience with QuickBooks, both cash-basis and accrual-basis, and with getting it to agree with your management platform — including how and when data moves between them, and who reconciles what.

Close and audit readiness

A fiscal close that's a process rather than a scramble, with the documentation your auditor asks for produced as a byproduct of normal work.

Sound familiar?

  • Your ShulCloud and QuickBooks totals don't match, and nobody is sure which one is right.
  • Income gets entered in two places, so reconciling takes too long.
  • The books are behind, and the cleanup keeps getting put off.
  • The security fund, the building fund, and the capital campaign all run through one income account, and no one can say what belongs where.
  • The person who set up the original general ledger left long ago, and the institutional memory left two staffers after them.

I'm a consultant, not your accountant or auditor — I work alongside them. What I bring is knowing how these frameworks have to be built inside synagogue management platforms specifically, which is a narrower and more unique problem than general nonprofit accounting.
