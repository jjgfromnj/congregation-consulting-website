Where congregations get stuck

Congregations reach me at every stage: before they switch, a couple of months in, and sometimes many years later. The sentence is almost always the same. It isn't supposed to be this hard.

ShulCloud is a capable platform. Most of the frustration I'm called in for isn't the software — it's a configuration decision made in year one that nobody has revisited, or a set of features the congregation is paying for and not using.

A setup nobody owns

Initial configuration is largely left to the congregation, and the person who did it has often moved on. Nobody now knows why the account types are the way they are, so nothing gets changed and everything gets worked around instead.

Member records that don't hold the truth

Yahrzeit dates entered against the wrong calendar, households that never got restructured after a divorce or a death, b'nei mitzvah dates living in someone's spreadsheet instead of the system. It surfaces as an embarrassing letter to the wrong person, usually at the worst possible moment.

Billing season is coming

Dues billing goes out once a year, and every mismatch in the member file goes out with it. I work through the run with your staff: checking account types against billing types, running a test billing that sends nothing, and only then sending statements.

Reports the board doesn't trust

Dues, pledges, and payments are recorded inconsistently, so the finance committee asks a simple question and gets three different answers.

Features paid for, never turned on

Integrated payments, SMS, Enterprise capability — sitting unused because nobody had time to evaluate whether they'd help or how to roll them out.

Migration coming up

Moving onto ShulCloud, or moving payment processing onto SIP, and the data cleanup is the part everyone underestimates.

How the work usually goes

A quick assessment first. I go through your ShulCloud site on my own, then we spend an hour on Zoom on what I found and what deserves attention — which is also your chance to tell me where the pain actually is.

Then the changes that matter. Reconfiguration and cleanup, starting with the demographic and membership records, and then the financial framework built on top of them — done with you rather than handed to you, so your team can maintain it.

Then training, if you want it. On your system, with your data, for your staff.

And implementation when you need it. Forms, emails, events, statements — the parts of ShulCloud that take longer than anyone has. When your office needs another pair of hands, I'm still here. I don't hand over a plan and disappear.
