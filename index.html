The fiscal year your office runs on

JUL
Fiscal year opens and dues billing goes out — and every error in the member file goes out with it.
AUG–OCT
High Holidays. Seating, tickets, the memorial book, the appeal, and a month with no room for a system that fights you.
NOV–DEC
School in full swing, yahrzeit notices going out, year-end giving, acknowledgment letters nobody needs to check.
JAN–FEB
B'nei mitzvah dates being set three years out. Statements and aging — who actually owes what.
MAR–APR
Pesach, the annual meeting, budget building, school registration opening — while last year's numbers are still being reconciled.
MAY–JUN
Renewals and dues letters, graduations, fiscal close, audit prep. Then it starts again.

A July start gives you the one real mercy in the calendar: the fiscal close lands in the quiet weeks after graduation, well clear of the Yamim Noraim. The strain sits at the front instead — billing, renewals, and the High Holidays inside ten weeks of each other.

Membership and lifecycle

The records the vendor didn't design for

A synagogue member file has to hold things no ordinary database anticipates: Hebrew names and yahrzeit dates that drift against the civil calendar, b'nei mitzvah scheduled three years out, families that have divorced and remarried, adult children aging off a household, interfaith and non-member families, cemetery plots, shiva, and pastoral notes. These records fail differently from financial ones. A bad number surfaces in a report, where someone can catch it. A bad record here surfaces as a yahrzeit that went unmarked, or a notice that never reached the family it was meant for.
